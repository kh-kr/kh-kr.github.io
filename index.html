About

I am an astrophysicist who loves to explore how stars are born, evolve, and sometimes break the rules — both individually and as members of clusters. Star clusters are natural laboratories that reveal the complex lives of stars, and my work focuses on how their members depart from canonical evolutionary pathways.

My research uncovers the physical mechanisms behind unusual stellar populations, such as blue stragglers, blue lurkers, and fast and slow rotater (intermediate-mass regime). By combining large-scale surveys with data-driven methods, I aim to understand how rotation, binarity, and stellar interactions shape the evolution of both individual stars and their host clusters.

I completed my Ph.D. in observational astrophysics at BITS Pilani, India (2023), with a thesis on blue straggler stars in open clusters.

Research

Blue stragglers & blue lurkers

Stars that appear younger than their cluster should allow -- rejuvenated by mass transfer, mergers, and collisions. I characterise these populations, their hot companions, and formation pathways using multiwavelength photometry from AstroSat/UVIT and Swift/UVOT to Infrared data, spectroscopy, and light curve modeling.

Dynamical ages of open clusters

Blue stragglers sink toward cluster centres as clusters relax, making them natural dynamical clocks. In a series of papers, I developed the use of the A+ parameter to measure the dynamical ages of open clusters, and also traced how they evolve toward dissolution.

Rotation & extended main-sequence turnoffs

Why do coeval stars fan out at the turnoff of young and intermediate-age clusters (younger than 2 Gyr age)? I investigate how rotation and braking of intermediate-mass stars (1.5--8 solar masses) sculpt extended main-sequence turnoffs across Galactic open clusters using N-body simulations, multiwavelength photometry, and spectroscopy.

Variable stars & eclipsing binaries

Time-series photometry from TESS, Kepler, and Gaia revealed eclipsing binaries and pulsators in clusters. I use light-curve modelling and radial-velocity monitoring of eclipsing blue stragglers constrain their masses, luminosities, and evolutionary state.

Machine-learning cluster membership

Reliable membership is the foundation of every cluster study. The ML-MOC, a kNN + Gaussian-mixture approach to determining open cluster members from Gaia data without hand-tuned cuts, now applied across dozens of cluster studies.
